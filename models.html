Which policy?

Every open-source robot policy, put in charge of the same humanoid, on the same tasks, with an identical adaptation budget for every pre-trained policy (20,000 steps; the from-scratch ACT control gets 60,000, since it starts from random weights) — scored from auditable simulated rollouts. ⚑ GR00T has a home advantage. It is the only entrant pre-trained on humanoids of this shape, so its lead means "best for this body", not "best in general". ACT is the control, not a contestant: 52M parameters, no pre-training at all, learning only from this task's 216 demonstrations. It is listed to show what the pre-trained models have to beat — and not one of the five general VLAs does. Only GR00T, pre-trained on this body, clears the from-scratch bar.
The pattern holds across all three tasks. GR00T leads every board and is the only policy to place anything — a 4% cylinder success, the suite's first. The 52M from-scratch control (ACT) still matches or beats every general VLA on pick-and-place and stack, and is edged only by WALL-OSS on cylinder, within error. Pre-training on internet-scale robot data has yet to clearly beat a from-scratch baseline on this humanoid — only pre-training on this body does.
And the models may not be the interesting variable. Five architectures separated by billions of parameters land inside each other's error bars at a fixed adaptation budget. That is a result about this suite, not a general law — but it is the reason we also grade the other input. The datasets benchmark freezes the policy and varies the data instead; its numbers come from a different robot and task, so the two are not on one scale.
