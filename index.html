What actually makes a robot policy better?

There are only two answers available: the model or the data it was trained on. Each gets its own benchmark here, built the same way — one embodiment, one protocol, an equal budget, and a deterministic score you can recompute from the logged rollouts.

Benchmark 01 · Policies

Which model?

Every open-source policy in charge of the same Unitree G1 humanoid, with an identical adaptation budget. Seven entrants — five have run all three tasks, two are pick-and-place only so far. ~1,350 scored rollouts, error bars on everything.

Benchmark 02 · Data

Which data?

One task, one model, one recipe, and three recordings of the same job that differ only in who produced them. Frame budgets matched, so what is graded is quality rather than volume.

Results → How we grade data → Source study →
Read together, the two boards raise a question neither answers alone. On the G1 suite, five architectures separated by billions of parameters land inside each other's error bars at a fixed adaptation budget. On RoboMimic Lift, three corpora matched to within 1% on frames span 34.83 to 4.85. These are separate experiments on different robots, tasks and scoring functions, so the two spreads cannot be put on one scale and no ratio between them is claimed. What they jointly suggest — and what we intend to test directly rather than assert — is that data quality may be the larger lever at a fixed budget. The experiment that would settle it is varying both on one embodiment.

Scores are not comparable across benchmarks — different robots, tasks, scoring functions and protocols. Each is ranked only within itself.

27 Jul 2026Result

New benchmark — datasets. Three demonstration sources, one model, matched frame budgets

The site now grades training data as well as policies. Three recordings of RoboMimic Lift — one expert teleoperator, six mixed-skill operators, and an RL agent — fine-tune the same GR00T N1.7 checkpoint with the same 1,000-step recipe on frame-matched corpora, scoring the best checkpoint per corpus. Mean progress spans 34.83 / 24.20 / 4.85, and the machine-generated corpus returns zero successes in 100 attempts — indistinguishable from a random policy on success rate. Diagnosis located a single bottleneck (grasping), and comparing across corpora showed it was a recipe defect rather than a data gap — worth +5.9 points from one inference-time parameter, no retraining.

27 Jul 2026Result

π0.5 completes the suite; stack & cylinder re-scored at an equal horizon

π0.5 finishes stack and cylinder, so all five phase-0b policies now carry a full combined score. Auditing the two new tasks surfaced an unequal evaluation horizon — GR00T and WALL-OSS had run 600-step episodes against 900 for everyone else — so both were re-run at the common 900-step budget. The fix lifted GR00T's combined score to 31.1 and revealed the first non-zero success on either new task: GR00T places the cylinder in 4% of episodes, invisible at the shorter horizon. WALL-OSS now edges the ACT control on cylinder (within error); ACT still ties for second overall.

21 Jul 2026Method

Scoring moved from milestones to continuous placement progress

The old score averaged three stages, two of which were saturated — every policy lifted the block, almost none placed it — leaving all five inside a 9.4-point band. Scoring the block's actual distance to the target, log-scaled, widened that to 13.4 points and cut the error margins from ±13 to ±3.

21 Jul 2026Method

WALL-OSS re-run after an unequal-horizon defect

Its earlier episodes ran 450 steps against 600 for every other policy — 25% less time on a task whose failure mode is running out of clock. Re-run at the common horizon it reached 0.7% success where it had scored 0%. Horizon is now asserted per policy and checked automatically at aggregation.

What's next

2

More task categories

Pick-and-place, stacking and cylinder are live. Two further categories are designed and awaiting scenes — even three tasks cannot support a general capability claim.

±3

Tighter margins

At 150 episodes we resolve gaps of about 4 points (the two newest entrants ran 50). The tied models need either more episodes or a more discriminative task to separate.

20

Power for the curation test

Selecting demonstrations by the screen beat a random draw by 10.9 points, but at n = 20 the interval crosses zero. It is the most commercially load-bearing claim on the site and the weakest evidence.

1

A second reference model

Every dataset grade so far is measured through GR00T N1.7. A grade that only holds for one architecture is a property of the model, not of the data.

RoboBench is an independent benchmark. Results are simulation-only and carry no claim about real-robot transfer. Every score here comes from logged, per-episode rollouts.
