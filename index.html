Pre-training on internet-scale robot data has yet to clearly beat a from-scratch baseline on this humanoid — only pre-training on this body does.

27 Jul 2026Result

π0.5 completes the suite; stack & cylinder re-scored at an equal horizon

π0.5 finishes stack and cylinder, so all five phase-0b policies now carry a full combined score. Auditing the two new tasks surfaced an unequal evaluation horizon — GR00T and WALL-OSS had run 600-step episodes against 900 for everyone else — so both were re-run at the common 900-step budget. The fix lifted GR00T's combined score to 31.1 and revealed the suite's first non-zero success: GR00T places the cylinder in 4% of episodes, invisible at the shorter horizon. WALL-OSS now edges the ACT control on cylinder (within error); ACT still ties for second overall.

21 Jul 2026Method

Scoring moved from milestones to continuous placement progress

The old score averaged three stages, two of which were saturated — every policy lifted the block, almost none placed it — leaving all five inside a 9.4-point band. Scoring the block's actual distance to the target, log-scaled, widened that to 13.4 points and cut the error margins from ±13 to ±3.

21 Jul 2026Method

WALL-OSS re-run after an unequal-horizon defect

Its earlier episodes ran 450 steps against 600 for every other policy — 25% less time on a task whose failure mode is running out of clock. Re-run at the common horizon it reached 0.7% success where it had scored 0%. Horizon is now asserted per policy and checked automatically at aggregation.

What's next

2

More task categories

Pick-and-place, stacking and cylinder are now live. Two further categories are designed and awaiting scenes — even three tasks cannot support a general capability claim.

±3

Tighter margins

At 150 episodes we resolve gaps of about 4 points (the two newest entrants ran 50). The five tied models need either more episodes or a more discriminative task to separate.

1

The stopping problem

GR00T locates the target best and then carries the block back out again. A halt-when-close rule is the cheapest experiment on the list.

RoboBench is an independent benchmark. Results are simulation-only and carry no claim about real-robot transfer. Every score here comes from logged, per-episode rollouts.
