Which data?

The model leaderboard holds the data fixed and varies the policy. This one does the opposite: one task, one model, one recipe, and three recordings of the same job that differ only in who or what produced them. Frame budgets matched, so what is measured is quality, not volume.

Same model, same recipe, same 1,000-step training run, same held-out evaluation seeds. One per-corpus choice is made and disclosed: the checkpoint scored is the best of {250, 500, 750, 1000} for that corpus, picked on a separate seed set and re-scored on fresh ones — PH @500, MH @250, MG @500. MG produces zero successes in 100 attempts, indistinguishable from a random policy on success rate (Wilson upper bound 3.7%); on mean progress it sits just above the floor rather than on it — 3.8–6.0 against 2.0–3.5. The best configuration found anywhere in this study, 43.83, is not plotted here: it is a 20-scene annealing experiment whose interval crosses zero, and it belongs in §6 with its caveat attached, not on the hero chart.

Progress = robosuite's staged task progress, 0–100: reaching scores 0–30, a completed grasp jumps to 60, and lift height interpolates 60–100. Interval = 95% bootstrap. Full protocol →

The headline. Three corpora recorded on the same task, matched to within 0.9% on frame count, produce policies spanning 34.83 to 4.85. Nothing about the model, the recipe or the evaluation changed. On this task the source of the demonstrations mattered more than the differences between the five general policies that sit inside each other's error bars on our policy leaderboard — though that board runs a different robot, task and scoring function, so the two spreads are not directly comparable and no ratio between them is claimed here.

1 · Three sources, one task, matched budgets

All three corpora are recordings of RoboMimic Lift. They are matched on frames, not episodes — episode-matching would confound quality with volume, because a sloppy operator produces longer episodes and therefore more training signal for the same nominal count.

Read the last two columns together. To supply the same 9,700 frames, MG needs a third as many episodes as PH because each one runs three times as long — and only a quarter of them finish the task at all. The corpus is mostly a recording of failure.

"Episodes used" is the frame-matched slice, not the size of the underlying recording. The MH recording is larger than the 94 episodes drawn here, which is what makes the selection experiment in §6 possible.

3 · Diagnosis — where the best policy breaks

Progress is a scalar, which is enough to rank and not enough to act on. Decomposing the task into reach → grasp → lift → place and conditioning each stage on the one before it turns the score into a funnel, and the funnel has one obvious constriction.

Reaching is largely solved. Lifting, once the object is held, is free — it never fails. Everything the policy cannot do lives in one transition: closing the fingers on an object it has already reached. If grasping succeeded every time it were attempted, the funnel implies an upper bound of 0.564 success against the observed 0.150. That is a ceiling on this decomposition, not a forecast of what any fix would deliver — the actual fix found in §5 recovered a fraction of it.

The physical version of that table: failures stall 4.7 cm short of the cube; successes get to 1.6 cm. The policy is not confused about where the object is — it stops three centimetres too early and closes on nothing.

c Confirmatory only. Closest approach and approach completion are downstream of success — an episode that succeeds must have got close — so they describe what failure looks like and cannot be prescribed on without circularity. Only path efficiency carries independent validation as a selection criterion (odds ratio 5.42, interval 2.49 – 11.81).

Note on two sets of transition rates. The funnel above is measured over the 100-scene run; the per-corpus table in §4 comes from the separate cross-dataset pass and reports somewhat different values for the same transitions (reach .91 vs 0.97, place-given-lift .65 vs 0.40). They are different estimates of the same quantities on different rollout sets, and the study does not reconcile them. Both are shown rather than silently picking the flattering one.

4 · Hardening — why the obvious prescription was wrong

Read from the PH policy alone, the diagnosis writes its own fix: grasping is the bottleneck, so go collect grasp demonstrations. That recommendation would probably have been wrong, and it took a second dataset to find out.

Place | lift0.400.670.00tracks the data

Reaching tracks the data source cleanly — better demonstrations, better reaching. Grasping does not. Two corpora with 100% successful demonstrations, recorded by different people to different standards, give grasp rates of 0.17 and 0.14, and at this sample size that difference is not resolvable (difference +0.04, interval −0.18 to +0.23, p = 0.73).

The rule this produced — stated as strongly as the evidence allows. A bottleneck that clearly moves with the data source is a data problem. A bottleneck where no difference is detectable across good corpora is a candidate recipe problem, and worth testing as one before spending a collection budget. That is a failure to detect a difference, not proof the corpora are equivalent — the point estimates still differ by a factor of 1.2 and n = 100 per arm cannot resolve rates this low. What makes the reading credible here is that the recipe fix in §5 then worked.

5 · The free fix

If the grasp failure is in the recipe, it should be reachable without retraining. It was. Executing four actions per inference call instead of eight — the policy re-plans twice as often as it acts on stale predictions — moved the grasp rate from 23% to 32% and progress from 34.83 to 40.71, +5.9 points.

No new data, no new training, no new model — one inference-time parameter. Two honest qualifications: the 40.71 arm was scored on 50 scenes against the baseline's 100, and four was selected from a sweep over action-step counts, so it is the best value found rather than an independently confirmed one. No interval is reported on the +5.9 in the source, which by this site's own standard it should be.

6 · Testing the prescription

A dataset grade is only worth something if acting on it works. So the screen was run forward — but with one of its properties, not all six. Selection used the only criterion with independent validation, path efficiency, penalised by action jerk: score = z(path efficiency) − 0.5 × z(action jerk). The top-scoring MH demonstrations were added to PH and compared against a random draw of the same frame count.

The baseline column is the one to read, and it is not flattering. Against doing no curation at all, targeted selection gains +3.12 progress, +3.0 grasp and +1.0 success — not the +10.89 headline. Most of the gap against the control is the control falling 7.78 points below the baseline: adding 98 unscreened demonstrations to a good corpus made it worse, despite doubling the frame count. The defensible claim from this experiment is "unscreened additions can hurt", which is useful; "screened additions help a lot" is not established.
And it is not significant. The 95% interval on the +10.89 difference runs from −7.53 to +28.94 at n = 20 scenes. This study has already watched one small-sample direction evaporate — spatial coverage read +0.578 at n = 30 and −0.021 at n = 100. A 14-to-6 split at n = 20 is not yet distinguishable from that pattern. It is a direction worth more rollouts, and that is the whole claim.

7 · What it amounts to — the screen

The point of grading a dataset is to be able to reject one before paying to train on it. These six properties are computable from the raw corpus on a laptop. Thresholds are set from the measured behaviour of a corpus that works.

Demonstration acceptance thresholds
PropertyAccept ifWhy that line
Task accomplishment≥ 0.95MG at 0.25 trained to near the random floor
Gripper transitions≤ 2One decisive close; MG averages 26.6 (length-normalise before applying)
Path efficiency≥ 0.45Odds ratio 5.42, interval 2.49 – 11.81 — the only independently validated line
Action jerk≤ 0.10MG's corpus jerk is 0.353
Grasp timing≥ 0.50Close on arrival, not at 15% of the episode
Episode length≤ 80 framesDecisive, not wandering — but see the false reject below
Applied to these three corpora, the screen is right about MG and wrong about MH. It rejects MG on all six properties and passes PH on all six. MH passes five and is rejected by the ≤ 80-frame rule at a median of 96 — yet MH trained to 24.20, the second-best policy here. That is a false reject on the second-best corpus, and it is the strongest evidence on this page that the length threshold is mis-set.

The screen was also calibrated on these same three corpora, so its agreement with them is a consistency check rather than a prediction. Whether it holds on a corpus it was not built from is untested — and that test, not the thresholds themselves, is what would make this a product.

8 · Negative results

Reported because they cost real compute and would otherwise be repeated:

  • Longer training was worse, not better. On PH the learning curve peaks at 500 steps (42.80) and falls to 29.37 by 1,000 — the model overfits a 9,666-frame corpus well before the nominal budget ends. This is why the headline scores use the best checkpoint per corpus rather than the final one, and why 500 steps is the configuration the study recommends.
  • Spatial coverage does not predict anything. Where the cube started in a demonstration read +0.578 at n = 30 and −0.021 at n = 100. Collecting demonstrations to "cover the workspace" is not supported here — and the reversal is a standing warning about small-n directions on this page.
  • Subsetting an existing corpus made it worse. Curating down to a higher-quality subset of the data already in hand scored 37.58 against 40.71 for leaving it alone. Selection helps when it adds screened demonstrations; discarding frames costs more than the quality gains.
  • The first version of this benchmark measured nothing. It scored conformance rather than closed-loop capability and returned noise across every data mix. It was discarded and rebuilt on real rollouts. The published numbers are all from the rebuild.

More than one task

Power for the curation test

The forward test is the only claim that matters commercially and it is the weakest evidence on the page. It needs to run at a sample size where the interval can exclude zero.

One model was used to score every corpus. A grade that only holds for GR00T N1.7 is a property of the model, not of the data.
